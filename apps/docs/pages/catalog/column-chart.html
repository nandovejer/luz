How it works

A table of one head row of names and one row of figures. Each figure is the text of its cell, and the cell draws its column under it, from the foot up to --v, in --luz-chart-1, with the track in --luz-chart-track. The columns share the width evenly.

Work out --v from your data: the value divided by the top of the scale, and the same top for every cell. The inline style sets only that. With a strict style-src, set it in a rule of your own, such as .sat { --v: .25 }.

At the default text size, up to seven columns fit a 320 px screen. For more, or with larger text, put the table in a luz-table-wrap: when a figure does not fit, the table scrolls inside it.

Without JavaScript

It needs none. Without the stylesheet it reads as a table: a head row of names, then the figures.

Keyboard and ARIA

Nothing takes focus. A table keeps its roles, so a screen reader reads each figure with its name. The caption names the table and says the unit.

Do not hide the table: it is the data. The columns are a picture of it and carry no text of their own.

Contrast and focus are in the Design tab.

Design

When to use

Use it to compare a few values that have an order or a name, such as the days of a week or the months of a year, on one scale.

When not to use

Do not use it for one value (use a meter), for long names (use a bar list), or for a trend over many points. Keep a table when exact values matter most.

Do and don't

Writing the content

Name the unit and the period in the caption (“Parcels per day, this week”), keep each name to a word or two, and write the figure with its unit when it is not obvious. The data and the labels are yours: the component only draws them.
