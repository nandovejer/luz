Known issues

Four things to know before you rely on it.

  • Without interactive there is no readout on hover or focus: the figures are in the table, which the button shows. With it, the readout follows the pointer and the focus alike.
  • It reads the table when it connects, when the table changes and when the box changes size. A table added to the page later, after the page loaded the script, needs the element to be added with it.
  • The plot is drawn left to right in every language, as a time axis is; the table, the title, the legend and the button follow the page’s direction.
  • It has not been tested with a screen reader.
Code Design

Code

Copy the markup

Write the table first, as the page a reader lands on without JavaScript. The first column is the X axis, a th of its row; each other column is a series, named by its th. Put each number in a <data value> and write it as you want it read in the cell. Wrap the table in a .luz-table-wrap only when it can be wider than the screen, as the three-series example does, and give each td a data-label so that on a narrow screen its rows stack and still name their cells. A table that always fits needs no wrapper: with one, the group, the region and the table would read the same name three times. The chart hides the wrapper with the table, and it takes no Tab stop meanwhile.

catalog.min.js loads the element’s own file, chart.min.js, when it scans the page; a page without catalog.min.js loads that file on its own. Link components/chart.css, after core’s.

How it works

CSS from @luz-ds/catalog and a file of its own, @luz-ds/catalog/chart (dist/chart.min.js, under 5 kB compressed), which defines <luz-chart>. It uses no library and draws with DOM calls only, so a page that forbids inline scripts and styles runs it as it is. To compare values by length with no script, use the bar list, the column chart, the stacked bar or the heat map.

  • The table: it stays in the page. While the chart shows, the table is moved off screen, not hidden, so a screen reader reads it as it always did; when the button shows the table, the drawing goes. On paper, the table prints and the drawing does not.
  • The drawing: an SVG marked aria-hidden, drawn again when the box or the table changes. It has no animation. Series are --luz-chart-1 to --luz-chart-6, so a change of mode repaints nothing.
  • Telling series apart: each line ends in a mark of its own shape (a circle, a square, a triangle, a diamond, a plus and a cross), repeated in the legend. In forced colors every line is the text colour, each with its own dashes.
  • The numbers: the scale starts at zero, or lower when a figure is negative, in round steps (1, 2 or 5 times a power of ten). The axes are written with Intl.NumberFormat in the page’s language, such as 1.2K.
  • Interactive: the header has a toggle button per series, with its name and the sum of its figures. The total is a sum: it suits counts and amounts, such as visitors or euros, and not rates, shares or averages, whose total says nothing. A line turned off is not drawn, on the same scale, and the last line shown cannot be turned off. The day under the pointer, or the row with the focus, gets a light band and a readout with its name and the figure of each line shown, copied from the cells.
  • The words: the script writes none of its own. The button’s two words are yours, in data-luz-label-*; the title and the legend copy your caption and your column heads.

Without JavaScript

The table, or the list, is all there is, and it is complete: the caption, every figure, every name. The element is a block and the button does not exist. With interactive there is no header and no readout: every figure is in the table. It is the same when the page lacks the table, or type is not one of the two.

Keyboard and ARIA

  • Keys: Tab reaches the “Show table” button; Enter or Space presses it. Without interactive nothing in the picture takes the focus.
  • Interactive: the buttons of the header are toggle buttons (aria-pressed): Enter or Space turns a line off and on, and the last line shown stays on. The picture then takes the focus, one Tab stop. It is a group named by the caption and holds a polite status that, on each key, says the label of the day, the same text as the row of the table. Each row is named by its own cells, from your text, “Apr 1, Desktop 252, Mobile 148”, every series, shown or not. With the focus on the picture, the arrow keys move to the next or the previous day (Right is always the next, as the plot is left to right), and Home and End to the first and the last; the keys of a screen reader’s browse mode are its own. Entering says its name and nothing else, and leaving empties the status.
  • The readout: its title is the name of the day, so the band is never the only sign of where you are. Escape puts it away without moving the pointer or the focus; it comes back on another day. It is hidden from assistive technology, which reads the row. A line turned off is not drawn, but its cells stay in the table, read as before. The script writes the roles of the table (table, rowgroup, row, columnheader, rowheader and cell) so that the rows keep them in every browser.
  • Name: the element is a group named by the table’s caption, and the button is described by it, so “Show table” is heard with what it shows. The drawing, its title and its legend are aria-hidden: the table says all of it.

Contrast and focus are in the Design tab.

Design

When to use

Use it to show how a few series change along an ordered axis, such as months, when the shape of the change is what the reader should see and you already have the numbers in a table. Add interactive for many days and two or more series, when the totals matter and a day is read one at a time. The small line (type="spark") is for a figure that needs its trend beside it.

When not to use

Do not use it when exact values matter more than the shape: a table says them better. For a few values compared on one scale, use a column chart or a bar list, which need no script.

  • Do not use it for parts of a whole: use a stacked bar. There is no pie or donut.
  • Do not use interactive for rates, shares or averages: the total in its header is a sum.
  • Do not use it for more than six series, or for series that cannot share a scale: split them into two charts.

It replaces a charting library that draws a canvas with no text beside it. Nothing in LUZ is removed.

Do and don't

Writing the content

The caption is the title of the chart, and says what is counted, where and when: “Parcels per route, January to June”. It is the name a screen reader gives the group.

  • Name each column as the legend should read, with its unit if it has one, and write each figure in its cell as you want it read: 1,204, not 1204, with the plain number in the value.
  • Write the button as the action and the thing: “Show table”, “Show chart”. Set both on the page, once, in its language.
  • With interactive, the head of each column is also the name of its button: a word or two.
  • Keep the names of the rows short: they are the labels of the X axis, and some are dropped when they do not fit.

Measured from the token values of each mode, rounded down. Needs is the WCAG 2.2 AA minimum: 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for the lines and marks; the grid is decorative and keeps a hairline of 1.3:1 or more.

  • Focus: the “Show table” button is core’s ghost button and takes its focus ring. The picture takes none. With interactive, a button of the header takes the focus ring, --luz-focus-ring, 2px, inside its edge, and so does the picture: the ring is drawn around it, 2px from its edge, because it is the picture that has the focus.
  • Target and focus: each button of the interactive header is more than 24 by 24 px (WCAG 2.2, 2.5.8), and the readout is placed beside the day that has the focus and never over it (2.4.11). Both criteria are new in WCAG 2.2 and not yet required by EN 301 549 v3.2.1.
  • A series is told by its colour, by the mark it ends in and by the legend; in forced colors every line is the text colour, with its own dashes, the area is not filled and the grid is the grey text colour. A series turned off has a hollow dot and a lighter name, and the band of a day is a dashed edge in forced colors.
