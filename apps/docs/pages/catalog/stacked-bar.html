Variants and options

OptionEffect
--v
On each part, its weight: the bar always fills its width and a part takes its --v of the sum, so 62, 21 and 9 work as well as .62, .21 and .09
--luz-stacked-bar-color
Another chart colour for a part and its item, such as var(--luz-chart-4)
--luz-stacked-bar-h
The height of the bar

How it works

A figure with two lists of the same shares in the same order. The legend is the data: a name and a figure in text for each share, with a swatch in its colour. The bar is a picture of it, a row of parts that take the width in proportion to their --v, with the surface showing through a thin gap between them.

The series take --luz-chart-1 to --luz-chart-6 in the order of the markup, so up to six shares keep their own colour. Put the largest first and group the smallest as “Other”. The inline style sets only --v. With a strict style-src, set it in a rule of your own.

Without JavaScript

It needs none. Without the stylesheet the figure reads as its caption and the legend, a list of names and figures; the bar is empty.

Keyboard and ARIA

Nothing takes focus. The caption and the legend are text: hide the bar with aria-hidden="true", as in the example.

In forced colors the parts and the swatches of the legend carry the same number, so a part is matched to its item by number. The number may not fit in a very narrow part; the legend is in the same order as the bar.

Contrast and focus are in the Design tab.

Design

When to use

Use it to show how a whole is split, when the parts add up to one total: statuses, sources, shares of a budget.

When not to use

Do not use it to compare many values (use a bar list or a column chart), for more than six shares, or for one share (use a meter).

Do and don't

Writing the content

Name the whole and the period in the caption (“Parcels by status, this week”), keep each name to a word or two, and write every figure with its unit. The data and the labels are yours: the component only draws them.
