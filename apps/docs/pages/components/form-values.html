Meter

A bar for a value in a known range, such as storage used, drawn next to its figure.

6 of 10 GB
Code Design

Code

Copy the markup

A native <meter> with its range. The text inside is for browsers that do not draw it:

HTML 
<div class="luz-field">
  <label class="luz-field__label" for="storage">Storage used, 6 of 10 GB</label>
  <meter class="luz-meter" id="storage" min="0" max="10" value="6">6 of 10 GB</meter>
</div>

Variants and options

  • min, max, value: the range and the figure.
  • Do not set low, high or optimum: the bar is always the brand colour, never a green or a red.

How it works

A native <meter> drawn by components/meter.css as an 8 px bar: a brand fill on a muted track with the field's border colour. The bar is a picture of a number that is already text. See the Meter page for tables and lists.

Without JavaScript

The bar is drawn and the figure is in the label. Nothing changes without your script, which sets value.

Keyboard and ARIA

  • A meter takes no focus and has no keys.
  • Give the figure and the unit in the label, or hide the bar with aria-hidden="true" when the figure is next to it.

Contrast and focus are in the Design tab.

Design

When to use

Use a meter for a value inside a known range: a share, a count against a maximum.

  • Storage used, seats taken, a score out of ten.
  • Write the figure next to the bar: the bar alone is not a number.

When not to use

A meter is a gauge, not a task in progress.

  • How far a task has got: progress.
  • A value the reader sets: a range.

Do and don't

Writing the content

  • Label it with the figure and its unit: "Storage used, 6 of 10 GB".
  • Say what is measured, not what the bar looks like.
  • Never rely on the colour of the bar: it is the brand colour in every state.

Contrast and focus

Output

The result of a calculation or of a control, such as the number beside a slider.

Code Design

Code

Copy the markup

A native <output>; for lists the ids of the controls it comes from:

HTML 
<p>Total: <output class="luz-output" id="total" for="qty price">12</output> seats</p>

Variants and options

  • .luz-output: the face of the interface, the brand colour, figures of equal width, at least 2.5 em wide and aligned to the end.
  • A bare <output> with no class: a small chip in the mono face.

How it works

A native <output>. Your script writes its value; LUZ does not calculate. An output is not sent with the form, and its implicit ARIA role is status.

Without JavaScript

The output shows the value written in the HTML. Nothing recalculates without your script.

Keyboard and ARIA

  • An output takes no focus and has no keys.
  • Tie it to its sources with for, and put the unit in the text around it.

Contrast and focus are in the Design tab.

Design

When to use

Use an output for a value that your page calculates or that a control sets, shown as text.

  • The number beside a range, a total, a result.
  • Write the unit next to it: "12 seats".

When not to use

An output shows a value; it does not collect one.

  • A value the reader types: an number or a text field.
  • Text that never changes: a paragraph or a span.

Do and don't

Writing the content

  • Put the figure in the output and the unit in the text around it.
  • Label what is counted: "Seats", "Total".
  • Keep the text short: an output is for a value, not a sentence.
